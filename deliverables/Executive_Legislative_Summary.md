# Virginia Clean Energy Compliance Pathways: Executive Summary

*A cost analysis prepared for the Virginia Department of Energy — working draft*

---

## Why this analysis exists

The Virginia Clean Economy Act (Va. Code §56-585.1(A)(6)/§56-1706.1) and the
Renewable Portfolio Standard (§56-585.5) set a legally binding path for
Dominion Energy Virginia to reach 100% clean electricity generation by 2045.
Both laws were enacted in 2020, based on the demand projections available at
that time. Electricity demand in Virginia — driven substantially by data
center growth — has since roughly doubled, and continues to grow. This
analysis independently models what full compliance actually costs under
today's real demand trajectory, not the smaller one the law was originally
written against, and compares that cost against two alternative paths.

## Three pathways examined

- **Scenario 1 — Full compliance.** Solar and battery storage only, no new
  gas generation, reaching genuinely zero-carbon generation by 2045 as the
  statute requires.
- **Scenario 1B — Full compliance with a narrow exception.** Identical to
  Scenario 1 through 2044; allows up to 5% gas generation from 2045 onward.
  A relevant finding below: this exception turns out to matter far less in
  practice than its 5% headline figure suggests.
- **Scenario 2 — Minimum statutory build only.** Builds only the specific
  solar and battery capacity the statute names as a minimum (16,100 MW
  solar; 16 GW short-duration and 4 GW long-duration storage), relying on
  the existing and expanded gas fleet for the remaining generation need.

## The headline finding

*(A note on this table's own history: an earlier version of this document briefly reported Scenario
2's direct cost as close to or below Scenario 1's. That finding did not hold up — a real accounting
error in Scenario 2's own gas-turbine cost figure was found and corrected after this document was
first drafted, and the corrected numbers below reflect that fix.)*

| | Direct cost to ratepayers | Full societal cost (adds climate and health impacts) |
|---|---|---|
| Scenario 1 (full compliance) | $42.45/MWh | $89.30/MWh |
| Scenario 1B (5% gas exception) | $42.14/MWh | $89.40/MWh |
| Scenario 2 (minimum build) | $43.77–$49.43/MWh* | $121.33–$126.99/MWh* |

*\*Range reflects three independent natural gas price forecasts (Deloitte, U.S. Energy Information Administration, and Wood Mackenzie/Hughes); see the technical summary for detail.*

**Scenario 2 costs more than either compliance pathway on both measures** — roughly $1-7/MWh more on
the direct, ratepayer-facing cost, and $32-38/MWh more on the full societal cost. **The full
societal cost gap is the larger, more consequential number**: Scenario 2's total cost — including
the value of climate and public-health harm from sustained gas generation — runs substantially
higher than either compliance pathway, driven by both a real (not marginal) direct-cost difference
and, more heavily, by climate cost from sustained gas generation through 2045.

Scenario 1B's own 5% gas allowance for 2045 turns out to matter little: the
physical gas fleet itself, by the VCEA's own retirement schedule, is too
small by 2045 to generate anywhere near 5% of demand even when the law would
permit it. In practice Scenario 1B ends up nearly identical to full
compliance, on every measure.

## Two figures specifically worth the General Assembly's attention

**Social Cost of Carbon.** Virginia law (§56-598(2)(d)/§56-585.1(A)(6))
specifically directs the State Corporation Commission to consider a
Virginia-specific social cost of carbon when evaluating new generation. No
Commission-set rate currently exists; this analysis uses the U.S. EPA's own
published rate as the best available substitute, and reports it as its own,
separate figure — not folded into the broader climate-cost total above —
specifically because the statute calls it out by name:

| | Social Cost of Carbon (CO2 only) | Broader climate cost (CO2 + methane + nitrous oxide) |
|---|---|---|
| Scenario 1 | $39.84/MWh | $43.66/MWh |
| Scenario 1B | $40.19/MWh | $44.05/MWh |
| Scenario 2 | $66.16/MWh | $72.84/MWh |

**RGGI compliance cost.** Separately from the climate-cost estimates above —
which represent the broader value of harm avoided, not a bill ratepayers
actually pay — Virginia rejoined the Regional Greenhouse Gas Initiative on
July 1, 2026. This is a real, market-priced cost gas generators must pay for
every ton of carbon emitted, already reflected in electricity prices today.
Folding this in:

| | Direct cost without RGGI | Direct cost with RGGI |
|---|---|---|
| Scenario 1 | $42.45/MWh | $46.22/MWh |
| Scenario 2 (range across gas-price cases) | $43.77–$49.43/MWh | $51.48–$57.15/MWh |

RGGI's own market price has run well above its regulatory floor since
Virginia's re-entry, reflecting real scarcity — the figures above use RGGI's
own published floor schedule as a conservative reference point, not the
higher, current market price, and should be read as a plausible lower bound
rather than a guarantee that costs will stay this low.

Combining RGGI with the full societal-cost figure from the table above —
climate and health impacts plus this real, currently-billed carbon cost —
brings the total to roughly $93/MWh for Scenario 1 and $129–$135/MWh for
Scenario 2, depending on the gas-price case. RGGI does not change which
pathway costs more or by roughly how much; it adds a broadly similar
$4–5/MWh to every scenario's own total. Full detail in the technical
summary.

## Reading this summary

This is a working draft, and the underlying analysis continues to be
refined and expanded — including a further scenario examining distributed,
locally-sited solar not yet reflected here. The figures above represent
this project's own, independently verified modeling as of this draft; the
full technical basis, all assumptions, and every documented correction
along the way are available in the accompanying technical summary and the
full methodology appendices.
