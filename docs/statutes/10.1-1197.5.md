# § 10.1-1197.5. Definitions

**Title 10.1 (Conservation) › Chapter 11.1 (Department of Environmental Quality) ›
Article 5. Small Renewable Energy Projects**

Retrieved 2026-09-11 from law.lis.virginia.gov. Amended 2026, cc. 156 (HB508), 157 (SB340),
901 (SB645) — the agrivoltaics definition is recent.

> **As used in this article:**
>
> **"Agrivoltaics"** means the intentional co-location of agricultural production and solar energy
> generation on the same land that (i) is designed to prioritize and sustain agricultural
> productivity while simultaneously integrating renewable energy generation, (ii) allows the
> ongoing production and sale of marketable agricultural products throughout the solar array's
> life, (iii) is a part of a farm business that is consistent with commercial agricultural
> production, (iv) has provisions for decommissioning to protect the land's agricultural resources
> and productivity, (v) does not significantly displace farming activity, and (vi) ensures
> flexibility for farmers to adapt to market conditions and support operational needs.
>
> **"Small renewable energy project"** means (i) an electrical generation facility with a rated
> capacity **not exceeding 150 megawatts** that generates electricity only from sunlight or wind
> and its dedicated associated interconnection facilities; [... clauses (ii)–(vi) omitted, covering
> hydro/wave/tidal/geothermal at 100 MW, biomass/waste at 20 MW, electrochemical storage at 150 MW,
> hybrid projects, and a Planning District 8 provision]

---

## Notes for this analysis

**SCOPE IS ARTICLE-LIMITED.** The definition opens "As used in this article" — Article 5, Small
Renewable Energy Projects, which governs DEQ's permit by rule. It is **not** a general Code-wide
definition of agrivoltaics, and this analysis should not present it as one without checking whether
another provision adopts it by reference.

**THE 150 MW CEILING MATTERS.** A "small renewable energy project" is capped at 150 MW for solar.
The utility-scale buildout modelled here is far larger in aggregate, though individual projects
may fall under the cap. Whether this definition binds a given project depends on project sizing,
not on the programme total.

**CLAUSE (i) IS THE RISK TO THE ~ZERO SLCOE FINDING.** Standard-density single-axis tracking is
designed to prioritize ENERGY yield; the statute requires a design that "prioritize[s] and
sustain[s] agricultural productivity". A conventional array with sheep beneath it satisfies
(ii), (iii) and plausibly (iv) and (vi), but "prioritize" in (i) and "does not significantly
displace farming activity" in (v) are the clauses a conventional layout would be tested against.

This is a legal question about design intent and displacement thresholds, neither of which the
statute quantifies, and it is not resolved here.
